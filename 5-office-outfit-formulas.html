5 Outfit Formulas for Every Office Dress Code

Copy-and-paste outfit combinations for business formal, business professional, business casual, smart casual and casual offices.

9 min read

"Business casual" means something different at every company. One office expects blazers every day, and another is happy with jeans and sneakers. Getting it wrong is stressful, whether you show up underdressed for a client meeting or overdressed on your first day.

The fix is an outfit formula: a simple template you can repeat with different pieces. Learn one formula for each dress code and you'll never stand in front of your closet wondering what to wear again.

In this guide you'll find:

  • A quick way to read your office's real dress code
  • One formula for each of the five most common dress codes
  • Three ready-to-wear outfit combinations for every formula
  • Versions for women, men and modest dressing
  • The pieces that work across all five dress codes

First, decode your office dress code

The written policy is a starting point. The real dress code is what people actually wear. Before you plan your work wardrobe:

  1. Look at your manager and their manager. Dress to their level, not to the most casual person in the room.
  2. Check the company's LinkedIn and website photos. They show how the company wants to be seen.
  3. Notice what changes on meeting days. Many offices are casual day to day but expect more on client days.
  4. When in doubt, go one level up. You can always take off a blazer. You can't add one you didn't bring.

Here's how the five dress codes compare:

Dress code Feels like Typical workplaces
Business formal Board meeting, court, formal events Law, finance, government, senior executive roles
Business professional Polished every day Banking, consulting, accounting, sales
Business casual Smart but relaxed Most corporate offices, HR, marketing, healthcare admin
Smart casual Put-together without trying too hard Tech, media, agencies, startups
Casual / creative Comfortable and personal Design studios, small startups, remote-first teams

Formula 1: Business formal

The formula: Matching suit + crisp shirt or blouse + closed-toe leather shoes + minimal accessories

Business formal is the most traditional dress code. Everything should match, be tailored, and stay in dark, quiet colors. Fit matters more than anything else.

Colors to stick to: navy, charcoal, black, white, pale blue

Outfit combinations

# Women Men
1 Navy trouser suit + white silk blouse + black pumps + pearl studs Navy suit + white shirt + burgundy silk tie + black oxfords
2 Charcoal skirt suit (knee-length) + pale blue shell top + nude heels + slim watch Charcoal suit + pale blue shirt + navy tie + black cap-toe oxfords
3 Black sheath dress + matching black blazer + black block heels + small gold earrings Black suit + white shirt + silver-grey tie + black derby shoes

Modest option: A long tailored navy or charcoal suit jacket over wide-leg matching trousers, a high-neck blouse, and a hijab in white, cream or a soft neutral. A dark abaya with a structured cut also works in many formal settings.

Get it right:

  • Have your suit tailored. Sleeves should end at your wrist bone, and trousers should just touch the top of your shoes.
  • Keep jewelry small: studs, a watch, one ring.
  • Polish your shoes. People notice.

Formula 2: Business professional

The formula: Tailored trousers or skirt + blouse or shirt + blazer + polished shoes

Business professional is one step down from formal. You still wear a blazer most days, but the pieces don't have to match, and you can add a little color.

Colors to work with: navy, charcoal, camel, grey, white, plus one soft color like light blue, blush or burgundy

Outfit combinations

# Women Men
1 Grey tailored trousers + white blouse + navy blazer + black loafers Grey wool trousers + white shirt + navy blazer + brown leather oxfords (tie optional)
2 Black pencil skirt + blush blouse + camel blazer + nude block heels Navy trousers + light blue shirt + grey blazer + dark brown loafers
3 Navy sheath dress + grey blazer + black pumps + thin belt Charcoal trousers + white shirt + navy knit tie + black oxfords

Modest option: Wide-leg tailored trousers, a longline blazer that covers the hips, a long-sleeve blouse and a hijab in a coordinating neutral or soft color.

Get it right:

  • A blazer is your "uniform." Own at least two: one dark (navy or charcoal) and one lighter (grey or camel).
  • Blouses in silk-like fabrics look more professional than cotton tees.
  • Keep a spare blazer at your desk for surprise meetings.

Formula 3: Business casual

The formula: Smart trousers or skirt + knit, blouse or shirt + optional layer + loafers or flats

Business casual is the most common office dress code, and the most confusing. The rule of thumb: you should look ready for a meeting, but you don't need a suit. A blazer is optional, and knits are welcome.

Colors to work with: any neutral, plus soft or rich colors like olive, rust, burgundy or dusty blue

Outfit combinations

# Women Men
1 Camel wide-leg trousers + cream fine-knit sweater + brown loafers + gold hoops Khaki chinos + navy merino sweater over a white shirt + brown loafers
2 Navy midi skirt + striped button-down shirt + white leather flats + tan tote Grey trousers + light blue oxford shirt (no tie) + brown suede derbies
3 Black straight trousers + burgundy blouse + long grey cardigan + black ankle boots Olive chinos + white shirt + unstructured navy blazer + white leather sneakers (if allowed)

Modest option: A midi or maxi skirt with a long-sleeve knit, a long cardigan or kimono, and a hijab in a color that ties the outfit together.

Get it right:

  • The difference between business casual and casual is usually fabric. Swap a cotton tee for a fine knit, or jeans for chinos.
  • Dark, well-fitted jeans are acceptable in some business casual offices, often on Fridays. Check first.
  • Avoid anything you'd wear to the gym or the beach: no hoodies, leggings, flip-flops or graphic tees.

Formula 4: Smart casual

The formula: Dark jeans or chinos + elevated top + one structured piece + clean shoes

Smart casual is relaxed but intentional. Jeans and sneakers are fine, as long as there's at least one "smart" piece in the outfit, like a blazer, a crisp shirt or leather shoes.

Colors to work with: your full wardrobe, including patterns like stripes and checks

Outfit combinations

# Women Men
1 Dark straight jeans + white tee + navy blazer + white leather sneakers Dark jeans + white tee + navy blazer + white leather sneakers
2 Black trousers + striped long-sleeve top + camel trench + black loafers Grey chinos + navy knit polo + suede jacket + brown desert boots
3 Midi slip skirt + oversized cream sweater + ankle boots + crossbody bag Black jeans + grey crew-neck sweater + white shirt collar showing + black Chelsea boots

Modest option: Wide-leg jeans or trousers, a longline shirt or tunic, a denim or utility jacket, white sneakers and a printed or colorful hijab.

Get it right:

  • Use the "one smart piece" rule. A tee and jeans is casual. Add a blazer or leather loafers and it becomes smart casual.
  • Keep sneakers clean and minimal. White or black leather works best.
  • Fit is what keeps casual pieces looking polished. Baggy jeans and a stretched-out tee will look sloppy.

Formula 5: Casual and creative

The formula: Comfortable base + one personal statement piece + tidy finishing touch

Casual offices give you the most freedom, but "casual" still isn't "anything goes." Your outfit should be comfortable, clean and show some personality.

Colors to work with: anything, including bold colors and prints

Outfit combinations

# Women Men
1 Relaxed jeans + graphic or slogan tee (work-friendly) + oversized shirt jacket + chunky sneakers Relaxed jeans + plain heavyweight tee + overshirt + retro sneakers
2 Printed wide-leg trousers + fitted black top + leather slides + statement earrings Patterned short-sleeve shirt + cream chinos + canvas sneakers
3 Knit dress + denim jacket + white sneakers + colorful tote Hoodie under a wool coat + dark jeans + leather boots

Modest option: A printed maxi dress with a denim jacket, or relaxed trousers with an oversized shirt, plus a bold or patterned hijab as your statement piece.

Get it right:

  • Pick one statement per outfit: a print, a bold color or an interesting accessory. More than one can look chaotic.
  • Keep a "meeting kit" at work: a blazer and a pair of smart shoes for surprise client calls.
  • Clean and pressed still matters. Wrinkled clothes look careless at any dress code.

Final tips for dressing well at work

  1. Plan on Sunday. Pick your five work outfits ahead of time and you'll save time every morning.
  2. Use the same formula for a week. Repeating a formula with different pieces is how stylish people make dressing look easy.
  3. Invest in fit, not quantity. A few tailored pieces will do more for you than a closet full of almost-right clothes.
  4. Dress for your day. Check your calendar the night before. A client meeting means you go one level up.
  5. Comfort counts. If you're adjusting your clothes all day, you won't feel confident. Choose shoes you can walk in and fabrics that move with you.
