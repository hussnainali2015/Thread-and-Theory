# How to Spot Quality Fabric in Under 60 Seconds

*Six quick in-store checks that tell you whether a piece will last years or fall apart after a few washes. No fashion degree needed.*

You've probably done it: bought a sweater that looked great on the hanger, and three washes later it was pilled, stretched and fit for the back of the drawer.

The price tag won't always warn you. Expensive clothes can be badly made, and affordable clothes can be surprisingly good. What tells you the truth is the **fabric and how it's put together**, and you can check both in less than a minute, right there in the store.

In this guide you'll learn:

- The 60-second check you can do on any piece of clothing
- How to read a fabric label (and which fibers to look for)
- What good quality looks like for tees, knits, denim, shirts and coats
- Red flags that mean "put it back"
- How to check quality when you shop online

---

## The 60-second quality check

Do these six checks in order. Each one takes about 10 seconds.

### 1. Read the label (10 seconds)

Find the care label, usually on the inside left seam or at the back of the neck. Look at the **fiber content**.

- **Good signs:** natural fibers like cotton, wool, linen, silk and cashmere, or blends with a high percentage of them.
- **Fine in small amounts:** 2–5% elastane (spandex) adds stretch and helps a piece keep its shape. Some polyester or nylon in a blend can add strength.
- **Be careful:** 100% acrylic sweaters, and 100% polyester in pieces worn close to the skin. These often pill, trap heat and hold onto smells.

### 2. Hold it up to the light (10 seconds)

Hold the fabric up to a light or a window.

- **Good sign:** you can't see much light through it. The fabric feels dense.
- **Red flag:** you can clearly see your hand through a tee, sweater or pair of trousers. Thin fabric loses its shape, wrinkles faster and wears out quickly.

*Exception:* linen, chiffon, voile and lightweight summer fabrics are meant to be sheer. Judge them on the other checks.

### 3. Do the scrunch test (10 seconds)

Squeeze a handful of fabric tightly for five seconds, then let go.

- **Good sign:** the wrinkles mostly fall out, or soften within a few seconds.
- **Red flag:** it stays deeply creased. That's how it will look after an hour of sitting at your desk.

*Exception:* linen wrinkles by nature. That's part of its charm, not a quality problem.

### 4. Stretch and release (10 seconds)

Gently pull a small section of the fabric sideways, then let go. Try the cuff or hem of a knit.

- **Good sign:** it springs back to its original shape right away.
- **Red flag:** it stays stretched out, looks wavy, or you can see gaps between the threads. That piece will sag at the knees, elbows and neckline.

### 5. Check the seams (10 seconds)

Turn the piece inside out, or look closely at the side seams, and gently pull on each side.

- **Good signs:** small, tight, even stitches. No gaps when you pull. Seam edges are finished neatly so they won't fray.
- **Red flags:** large or uneven stitches, loose threads, or a seam that opens up when you pull it gently. Patterns (stripes, checks) that don't line up at the seams mean the maker cut corners.

### 6. Test the details (10 seconds)

Check the small parts that break first.

- **Zippers:** should run smoothly without catching. Metal zippers usually last longer than thin plastic ones.
- **Buttons:** should be sewn on tightly, with no loose threads. A spare button sewn inside is a good sign.
- **Buttonholes:** stitched densely, with no fraying.
- **Hems:** even and flat, not puckered or wavy.
- **Lining (on coats and blazers):** lies flat and isn't pulling at the edges.

**That's it.** If a piece passes all six checks, it's probably well made, whatever it costs.

## Know your fibers

Here's a quick guide to what's on the label.

| Fiber | Type | Pros | Watch out for | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cotton** | Natural | Breathable, soft, easy to wash | Can shrink or wrinkle | Tees, shirts, jeans, chinos |
| **Linen** | Natural | Very cool and breathable, gets softer with washing | Wrinkles easily | Summer shirts, trousers, dresses |
| **Wool / merino** | Natural | Warm, breathable, resists odors and wrinkles | Needs gentle washing | Sweaters, coats, trousers, suits |
| **Cashmere** | Natural | Very soft and warm | Cheap cashmere pills fast | Sweaters, scarves |
| **Silk** | Natural | Luxurious, light, breathable | Delicate, shows water marks | Blouses, scarves, linings |
| **Viscose / rayon** | Semi-synthetic | Drapes well, soft, affordable | Can shrink and weaken when wet | Blouses, dresses |
| **Lyocell / Tencel** | Semi-synthetic | Soft, strong, breathable, eco-friendlier | Can wrinkle | Shirts, dresses, trousers |
| **Modal** | Semi-synthetic | Very soft, resists shrinking | Can pill over time | Tees, loungewear, underwear |
| **Polyester** | Synthetic | Strong, holds its shape, dries fast | Not breathable, holds odors | Outerwear, activewear, blends |
| **Nylon** | Synthetic | Very strong and light | Not breathable | Outerwear, tights, blends |
| **Acrylic** | Synthetic | Cheap, light | Pills quickly, not warm, feels plastic | Best avoided as 100% |
| **Elastane / spandex** | Synthetic | Adds stretch and recovery | Breaks down with heat | 2–5% in jeans, trousers, tees |

**A simple rule:** Natural and semi-synthetic fibers breathe better and feel nicer. Synthetics add strength and stretch. The best everyday clothes often use a smart mix, like 98% cotton with 2% elastane in jeans, or 80% wool with 20% nylon in a coat.

## What good quality looks like, piece by piece

### T-shirts

- Thick enough that you can't see through it when held to the light
- 100% cotton, or cotton with a little modal or elastane
- Neckline springs back quickly when stretched
- Side seams run straight down, not twisted toward the front

### Sweaters and knits

- Dense knit with no big gaps between stitches
- Wool, merino, cashmere, cotton, or a blend mostly made of these
- Cuffs and hem spring back after stretching
- Seams are knitted together ("fully fashioned"), not just cut and sewn
- Rub two areas together, like under the arm. If it already looks fuzzy, it will pill fast.

### Jeans

- Heavier denim (it feels sturdy, not floppy)
- Mostly cotton, with no more than 2–3% elastane for comfort
- Double-stitched seams and rivets on the pocket corners
- Metal zipper and a solid metal button

### Shirts and blouses

- Fabric feels smooth and dense, not rough or papery
- Small, dense stitches along the collar and cuffs
- Buttons sewn on tightly, with a spare inside
- Collar lies flat and doesn't curl at the tips
- Stripes and checks line up at the seams and pockets

### Coats and blazers

- At least 50–60% wool for warmth (in wool coats)
- Lining is smooth and lies flat
- Shoulders are smooth and even, with no puckering
- Buttons are firmly attached, with spares included
- The coat feels a little heavy. Very light wool coats are often mostly synthetic.

## Red flags: put it back if you see these

- [ ] You can see your hand clearly through the fabric (except for sheer fabrics)
- [ ] It's already pilling or fuzzy on the shelf
- [ ] Loose threads or uneven stitching
- [ ] A seam opens up when you pull it gently
- [ ] Patterns don't line up at the seams
- [ ] The fabric stays stretched out after you pull it
- [ ] It stays deeply wrinkled after the scrunch test
- [ ] The label says 100% acrylic on a sweater meant to keep you warm
- [ ] The zipper catches or feels flimsy
- [ ] It smells strongly of chemicals

## How to check quality when shopping online

You can't touch the fabric online, but you can still investigate:

1. **Read the full product description.** Look for the fiber content and the fabric weight. For tees, 180 g/m² (GSM) or more is a good sign. For jeans, 12 oz or more feels sturdy.
2. **Zoom in on the photos.** Look for neat seams, aligned patterns and how the fabric hangs on the model.
3. **Read reviews for clues.** Search for words like "pilling," "shrank," "see-through," "thin" or "held up well."
4. **Look for close-up or detail shots.** Brands that are proud of their quality usually show their stitching and fabric texture.
5. **Check the return policy.** If you can't return it easily, think twice. When it arrives, do the 60-second check at home before you remove the tags.

## Make quality clothes last even longer

Good fabric only stays good with the right care:

- **Wash less often.** Air out sweaters, jeans and blazers between wears instead of washing them every time.
- **Wash cold and inside out.** It protects colors and reduces pilling.
- **Skip the dryer for knits and stretchy pieces.** Heat shrinks wool and damages elastane. Dry knits flat.
- **Fold sweaters, hang shirts and coats.** Hanging knits makes them stretch at the shoulders.
- **Use a fabric shaver.** Even good knits pill a little. A fabric shaver makes them look new in minutes.
- **Fix small problems early.** A loose button or a small open seam takes five minutes to fix now and is much harder to fix later.

| 1. Label | Read the fiber content | Mostly natural or good blends |

## The bottom line

Quality isn't about price or brand names. It's about fabric that holds its shape and construction that holds together. Once you know what to look for, you'll spend less money on clothes that don't last and more on pieces you'll wear for years.
